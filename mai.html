How the score works

At every sale with at least 20 lots, the top 10% of lots by value are its highlights (apex lots), up to 25. A lot's value is its high estimate, or its sold price where there is no estimate. Bring a Trailer holds no sales, so each completed month counts as one, and its top 25 results are its highlights. MAI measures how much of that top end each make holds; Q and R are shown alongside it.

P

Presence

The make's share of a sale's top-10% lots. If a 200-lot sale has 20 highlights and 5 of them are one make, that make's P is 25%. This is what the score is built on.

Q

Quality

Sold price divided by the auction house's high estimate, averaged over the make's sold apex lots that had one. 1.0 means cars sold right at the top of their estimate. Shown, not scored: most lots carry no estimate.

R

Sell-through

The share of the make's apex lots that actually sold. Shown, not scored: an unsold lot with no estimate can't be ranked, so R reads high at sales without estimates.

Combining events

MAI = Σ(rating × P) / Σ(rating of every sale)

A sale's rating is what it took to be a top-10% lot there, scaled so the highest sale rates 100. A highlight at a sale whose line is $5M counts 25 times one at a sale whose line is $200K. Summing over every sale makes MAI a make's share of all highlight value, so topping one small sale can't outrank being among the highlights everywhere.

Reading the numbers

  • Scores across all makes add up to 1. Compare makes against each other.
  • Click a make in the ranking to see which sales its score comes from, and the cars behind it.
  • Modern and Classic split the same highlights by the car's age when it sold: under 25 years is modern, 25 or more classic. Each view ranks makes by their share of that segment, so its scores add up to 1 too. Most modern highlights come from Bring a Trailer, most classic ones from the live houses.
  • Q is left blank for a make with no sold apex lot that had a high estimate.
  • A make with a handful of apex lots at one event can post a high R of 100%. Check the Events and Apex lots columns before reading much into it.

Where MAI fits

MAI measures network density (D) in the Utility Dividend framework: how embedded a make is among top collectors, auction houses and concours. The full framework is

UD = (Enjoyment × Usage) + Individual Nostalgia + (Collective Nostalgia × Nα × D × 1 / Survivorship)

Survivorship scales only the collective term, and is counted per distinct design rather than per edition name: a cosmetic special edition has the scarcity of its base model. D is measured at the make level and applied to each model.
