How the score works

For every auction event, MAI measures three things about each make's apex lots. It then multiplies them together, so a make only scores well if it is present, sells, and sells well.

P

Presence

The make's share of the apex lots at the event. If 40 apex lots cross the block and 10 of them are one make, that make's P is 25%.

Q

Quality

Sold price divided by the auction house's high estimate, averaged over the make's sold apex lots. 1.0 means cars sold right at the top of their estimate.

R

Sell-through

The share of the make's apex lots that actually sold. It reflects how confident buyers are at that price level.

Combining events

MAI = Σ(rating × P × Q × R) / Σ(rating)

Each event's P × Q × R is weighted by that event's auction rating, so a strong showing at a deep, high-value sale counts for more than one at a thin sale. The rating is 30% apex concentration, 40% apex sold volume and 30% apex sell-through, each scaled 0 to 100 across all events.

Reading the numbers

  • Scores are small because they multiply three fractions. Compare makes against each other, not against a fixed scale.
  • Q is left blank for a make with no priced sale. That case already shows up as a low R.
  • A make with a handful of apex lots at one event can post a high R of 100%. Check the Events and Apex lots columns before reading much into it.

Where MAI fits

MAI stands in for the network density term (D) of the Networked Utility Dividend framework: how embedded a make is in the circle of top collectors, auction houses and concours. The full framework is

U = (Fun × Exp) + Nostalgia + (CulturalSalience × Nα × D) × (1 / Survivorship)
